# Code-built history

Guidance for the `history` video type. It adds to codebuilt-script and codebuilt-direction; the word count and JSON shape in the prompt win.

## Sources first
- Every date, number, name and place needs a source you can name (museum, archive, encyclopedia, scholarly work). If you cannot name one, leave the claim out.
- Give a year or exact date only when sources agree. Where they differ or the record is uncertain, say "about", "around" or "by", or name the dispute in one line.
- Use the names and spellings sources use; give a person's role in a few words the first time (for example, "the Roman general Scipio"). Keep to 1 to 3 people per video.
- Do not invent dialogue, thoughts, motives, crowds or events. Where the record is silent, say so, or leave it.

## Shape
- Open with the stake or the surprising sourced fact, 12 words or fewer, with its year if it helps.
- Tell events in time order. Name the year when the place or the actor changes, so the viewer always knows when they are.
- Carry the story through 2 to 5 real places and return to them. A place is a map location, a building, a landscape or an object, not a stock scene.
- End on the most surprising consequence, as the sources state it. Keep the hook's promise, echo a word or picture from the hook, and cut on that sourced payoff; do not add an opinion or coda.

## Picturing it
- Maps are the main tool: use codebuilt-maps to show where, borders as they were only if the map data supports it, otherwise show modern outlines and label them as modern.
- Show people as simple figures or emblems, never as faces of real individuals; label reconstructions as reconstructions.
- Put the year on screen as a caption card at each change of time. Numbers and dates on screen must equal the narration exactly.
- Do not stage invented battles, crowds or ceremonies as if recorded; show the place, the object or the map, and move the camera through it.

## Check
- Before finishing, list each date, person and place with its source, and fix any that has none.
