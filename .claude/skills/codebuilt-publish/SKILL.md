# Code-built publish copy

The title and tags are written after the video is built, from the topic, the premise and the narration. They do not bring views on their own; they help YouTube's first step (deciding the topic, so the video reaches viewers who like it) and they must match what the video shows. The brief, the premise and the channel profile win over this text. Background: `dashboard/server/growth/knowledge/references/metadata-rules.md`.

## Title

- One concrete subject and its premise, as a question or a claim: "Why Chile is 4,300 km long but only 177 km wide", "What holds up a suspension bridge's main cables". Use the words the narration uses.
- About 40 to 70 characters, the subject near the front, in the channel's language. Hard limit 100; no `<` or `>`.
- A number or a date is welcome when it is part of the premise. Never a title that is only a date, a time or an id.
- Say what the video actually shows and answers. No "you won't believe", no all-caps, no emoji, no `#Shorts`, no hashtags.
- Not a template: do not start with "The truth about", "X explained", "Did you know". Each title is its own sentence.
- Fiction or what-if: say it is a what-if ("What if the Moon were twice as close").

## Tags

- 6 to 12, lowercase, no `#`, no spaces inside a tag (join words: suspensionbridge).
- About half name the exact subject (places, objects, people, events, and a common misspelling if there is one), a few the wider topic, two or three broad search terms.
- Never "fyp", "viral", "foryou", "shorts", "trending".

## What is not done here

- No upload and no publish: only the Publish button or auto release does that (CLAUDE.md rule 6).
- The description is built by the draft code from the premise answer and the narration, and the Publish code adds the AI disclosure and hashtags. Do not repeat it in the title.
