# Title, description, tags and first frame

Purpose of metadata on Shorts: help YouTube decide the topic (feed step 1) and tell a viewer what they will get. It is not a trick that brings views by itself [YOUTUBE].

Title
- States the premise as a question or a claim about one concrete subject, in the channel's language. Plain words a viewer would say.
- About 40 to 70 characters; the subject word near the front. YouTube's hard limit is 100 characters, and `<` `>` are refused.
- A number or date is welcome when it is part of the premise ("Why Chile is 4,300 km long"). A title that is only a date, a time or an id is refused by the Publish code.
- Never promise what the video does not show. No all-caps, no "you won't believe", no emoji walls.
- Do not end every title with `#Shorts`; it does nothing [YOUTUBE].
- Avoid a fixed template shared by many videos ("The truth about X", "X explained"): shared templates are what the template-content policy looks for [YOUTUBE].

Description
- First line (about 125 characters, what shows before "more"): the premise and the answer in one plain sentence, with the main subject named.
- Then the narration text (the Publish code adds it), sources for any number when the type needs them, and the AI disclosure line the Publish code adds.
- 3 to 5 relevant hashtags at most, at the end. Too many hashtags and YouTube ignores them [YOUTUBE]; they are not a reach lever.

Tags
- 8 to 12, lowercase, no `#`, no spaces inside a tag. Mix: about half the exact subject (names, places, things), a few for the wider topic, two or three broad search terms.
- Never "fyp", "viral", "foryou", "shorts": they do nothing on YouTube.
- Tags matter little on YouTube; spelling variants of the subject are the main use [YOUTUBE].

First frame (the feed's "thumbnail" for Shorts)
- The subject of the premise is visible and large in frame one, inside the 9:16 safe area (clear of the bottom 20% and right edge where the YouTube buttons sit).
- No black frame, no logo, no title card first.
- A custom thumbnail on a Short shows mainly on the channel page and in search, not in the swipe feed [CREATOR]; the first frame matters more.
