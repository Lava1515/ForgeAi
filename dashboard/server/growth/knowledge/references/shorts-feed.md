# How the Shorts feed picks videos

Source tags: [YOUTUBE] YouTube or its staff said it; [DATA] ForgeAi measured it (research 2026-11-14); [CREATOR] a creator or vendor claims it.

1. Understand the video. YouTube reads the title, description, speech, picture and language to decide the topic [YOUTUBE]. A plain title that states the premise and a real description help this step. A date, a draft id or a list of hashtags gives it nothing.
2. First plays. There is no fixed test batch: each time a viewer opens the feed, fresh videos compete by topic match [YOUTUBE]. One topic area per channel sends a clear signal; scattered topics send a weak one.
3. Stay or swipe. "Viewed versus swiped away" exists for every play and is the densest signal [YOUTUBE]. The first frame and the first sentence must show the premise.
4. Watch time. Average view duration and percentage viewed, adjusted for length [YOUTUBE]. As long as the idea needs and no longer; pay off the promise.
5. Satisfaction. Surveys and later behaviour carry a lot of weight; likes, comments and shares count but are sparse [YOUTUBE]. Deliver what the title promised.
6. Spread or stop. Good signals bring more viewers of the same kind, then wider. A Short that failed almost never revives: 0 of 1,546 old Shorts under 1,000 views gained another 1,000 [CREATOR]. Do not wait on a dead video; make the next one.

Not levers (YouTube staff denied them, and the data agreed): posting time, a posting-frequency threshold, hashtags, `#Shorts` in the title, "warming up" an account [YOUTUBE] + [DATA, 5,806 Shorts]. One weak video does not penalise the channel [YOUTUBE].

What does act on a whole channel is policy [YOUTUBE]: the monetization rule against mass-produced, template content; the spam rule against high volumes of near-identical automated videos; less reach for channels that mostly re-upload others' clips. So: no bursts of uploads within minutes, no duplicate uploads, no shared title templates across many videos, each video a distinct premise.

What the premise needs (31 winners against 10 flops, moderate strength) [DATA]:
- One concrete thing with a stake: a named place, object, person or number. No "things that..." lists.
- The premise is a full question or claim in the title; about half the winners also say or show it within 3 seconds.
- The subject is on screen in the first frame: no logo, no title card.
- End on the payoff and stop, or where a loop can start. Few winners had a call to action.
- No target for cuts per second or for length: winners ran 6 to 178 s.
